# LSAT 170 Curriculum — Style Guide

## Tone

Direct, second-person, no filler. Explain *why* a technique works, not just what it is. Prefer prose over bullet
lists for the teaching sections; use tables and lists for reference material (indicator words, decision matrices,
limits). Every page should read like a knowledgeable tutor talking to Stanley specifically — CS background,
working full-time, targeting a specific score for a specific financial reason.

## Required per-archetype structure

See `CONTRIBUTING.md` for the section-by-section breakdown. In short:

- **A/B** pages need a `cs-bridge` card mapping the day's logic onto software/CS concepts, and must name where the
  CS instinct misleads (over-formalizing wastes time; natural language isn't formal logic).
- **D** pages have no original questions — only `protocol`, `results`, and `scoring` sections.
- Every page with original items includes the originality notice verbatim: *"Practice items on this page are
  curriculum-authored in LSAT style; they are not official LSAC questions."*

## Copyright / accuracy rules (non-negotiable)

- Never reproduce official LSAC stimuli, questions, answer choices, or answer keys.
- Never claim a specific official question (by PT number + section + question number) is a specific type. Drill
  instructions say "scan the section, work only today's type" — LawHub's own metadata, not this site, identifies
  question types on official material.
- Never describe Logic Games / Analytical Reasoning as a current section of the LSAT — it was removed in August
  2024. It's fine to mention it existed historically, but always in clearly past-tense, no-longer-tested framing.
- PrepTest numbers referenced in drill assignments must come from `data/preptest-allocation.json`, never invented.

## Original practice items

- Each item needs a defensible single correct answer and a genuine reason the other choices are wrong — avoid
  writing items where two answers are both arguably correct.
- Keep the item count modest and the quality high; it's better to have 6 solid original items than 12 mediocre ones.

## Banned phrases

Avoid generic LLM padding: "in today's fast-paced world," "it's important to note that," "in conclusion." Avoid
restating the section heading as the first sentence of its own paragraph.
